# Decisive-claim check

Use this when a requested detail decides whether a business belongs in the sourced list. The output is an attributed **website claim**, never independent verification of trading, occupancy, stock or availability.

## Join the facts before counting a candidate

Discover by category and place first; test narrower attributes on opened candidate pages. A quoted street-address term in every search query can miss a business that publishes the address in its footer or contact page. For each candidate, check the **same named business** against the request:

| Question | Acceptable page evidence | Insufficient on its own |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identity | Its own page names the business, or a parent/network page clearly connects the local operator to the brand. | A similar name, unrelated directory entry or assumed franchise affiliation. |
| Offering | An opened service/product/store page explicitly describes the requested offering. | A broad industry label, search snippet, unrelated service or recruitment advert. |
| Place | An opened location/service page explicitly lists the requested street address, store, or serviced place. Label which it is. | A PO Box when a street address is requested; a statewide claim for a named town; a catalogue when a walk-in shop is requested. |

A single first-party page may support all three facts; otherwise open and link the specific pages that connect them. If the offering and place appear on different pages, confirm both pages refer to that business and that the service-area claim applies to the requested offering. Do not join an unrelated company, product, branch or network member to fill a missing fact.

## Check before saying a decisive detail is absent

1. Read the **whole relevant opened page**, including service categories, location text, footers and contact sections. A text-extraction window may omit content; use an available full-page opener or a safe targeted page search where possible.
2. If the fact remains unclear, spend **at most one additional safe attempt** on a targeted search for the business + exact offering/place, or on an alternative page opener. Open the resulting page before using it. Respect TLS errors, blocks, robots/consent restrictions and the user's scope; do not bypass them.
3. If still unresolved, mark the business an **unconfirmed clue** and explain the gap. Say “not found on the pages checked,” never “the business does not offer it” or “the business does not exist.” Stop when the bounded source budget is exhausted, even if the requested count is unmet.

The 9 October 2026 [public evaluation](https://github.com/JakeMarchin-Vincent/skills/blob/master/docs/aus-business-finder/EVALUATION.md) showed why this matters: an address on a Mildura pest-inspection page and a “Kitchen Exhaust” service category on a Toowoomba location page were overlooked. Those are historical case findings, not current claims about either business. A blocked Rockhampton store page did not prove the shop absent. These are examples of *where to look and how to report uncertainty*, not names to return in future searches.

## Copy addresses and contacts without alteration

A street address must be copied from an **opened first-party page**, not a search result or directory snippet. When a browser snapshot is abbreviated, inspect the full page or relevant contact section before reporting the address. If it cannot be found in opened content, omit the address and report only the supported service-area claim. A locality in a footer does not establish that the requested offering is available there.

A phone number or email is optional. Copy it from an **opened first-party page or its actual contact link**, character-for-character. Do not complete a domain, infer an email from a website, or change a suffix to look more plausible. If the visible text and `mailto:` disagree, omit the contact or explicitly attribute the discrepancy. The earlier evaluation caught an email changed from `.com` to `.com.au`; that candidate's other claims were not thereby verified.

## Final answer-level check

For every counted candidate, you should be able to point to opened URL(s) supporting identity, offering and the attributed place claim. Cite the exact opened page for each workshop, branch or address detail, even if another page supports the business's main service claim; omit an uncited detail rather than relying on a search snippet or a different page. Check requested exclusions and duplicates. Distinguish a published street address, advertised service area and website-described walk-in store; none proves current occupancy, coverage, operation or stock. Copy any contact exactly or omit it. Report the count of sourced candidates and separately list unresolved clues without padding.
